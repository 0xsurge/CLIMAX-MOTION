# Device study: TalkSign Marketplace (D1) and HeyClicky (D2)

There are two concept launch films, both 1138×640 at 30 fps. Both clips are kept locally in `v/` (git-ignored).
Contact sheets are in `sheets/`, at 1 fps for the whole film and 5–7.5 fps through each device.

| | D1, TalkSign Marketplace (sign-language data) | D2, HeyClicky (AI buddy for Mac) |
|---|---|---|
| length | 59.9 s | 35.1 s |
| voiceover | yes, the narration drives every beat | yes, the narration drives every beat |
| music | 136 BPM, steady energy, drops at 54 s for the URL | 136 BPM, softer, mixed at −19.8 LUFS |
| loudness | −13.3 LUFS | −19.8 LUFS |
| cuts | 21 (about 1 per 2.9 s), many soft | 7 (about 1 per 5 s) |
| cut to beat | median 0.13 s, no better than chance: cuts follow the voiceover, not the beat | same |
| ground | many: white, plum pixel collage, black, orange grid, pixel photos, colour-block frames, white | white, sky, desktop, sky, white wireframe |

## The big finding: captions are the voiceover, typed live
In both films, almost every line on screen is **the narration itself, appearing word by word in sync with the voice**.
- A word appears as it is spoken, and lines build up as the sentence runs.
- That is why the films feel "intelligent": they read like a person thinking aloud, and the visuals sit inside the
  sentence.

## D1 devices (in order)
1. **0–4 s: the inline media pulse.**
   - **Setup:** "0101010101 [ image ] Expression" sits in a row at the centre.
   - **The swap:** the middle slot changes image every 4 frames (a hand sign, a face, a group, a sticker, a gesture),
     with corner tick marks drawn on the frame.
   - **What it says:** data (binary) plus a human (image) gives a label.
   - **The rule:** a fixed sentence frame with a living slot.
2. **4–8 s: the collage burst.**
   - Many of the slot images explode outward into a layered collage, which floods into a plum pixel mosaic.
   - The text "collecting dataset…" types in the middle while binary labels float around.
3. **8–13 s: the visualised sentence.**
   - "While speech models learn": a waveform grows across the screen, and the voice drives it.
   - "from hundreds of thousands of hours of data": the words stack in little boxed tags under it.
   - **The image is the meaning of the sentence.**
4. **13–14 s: tagged words on footage.** The phrase stays as boxed label tags, pinned to corners over real footage.
5. **14–18 s: the image between two words (the "hand" moment the user saw).**
   - **Setup:** an orange grid ground; a card grows from a small dark slit.
   - **The line:** "that's why [ image of a hand signing ] accessibility AI / still leaves millions [ avatar stack ]
     behind."
   - **How it plays:** the images sit inline inside the sentence as a word-sized pill. Each word appears as it's spoken.
6. **18–21 s: pixel-sort reveal.**
   - "The problem isn't the technology / it's the data" plays over footage broken into large pixel blocks.
   - The blocks resolve, or sort, into the next shot through an orange cross-wash.
7. **21–27 s: colour-block frames.**
   - Footage sits inside nested rectangles of flat brand colour (orange, peach, lilac, black, plum) that keep
     rearranging: scaling, swapping, sliding.
   - Each new photo lands in a frame of the same geometry. It's a rhythmic frame dance with no camera move.
8. **27–29 s: logo type-on.** The mark, then "marketplace" types in on white.
9. **29–33 s: the product chip on the world.**
   - A brand chip ("● Contribute", "● Earn", "● Build better AI") sits centred on top of footage.
   - The word types each time, and the chip persists across three different worlds: people, a wallet UI, a daisy field.
10. **33–38 s: card stack, then card explosion.**
    - Real UI cards (dataset corpus, task list) grow and stack.
    - They then explode into dozens of tags (Legal, Finance, Education…) flying outward in 3D depth.
11. **40–47 s: particles orbit and merge into one form.**
    - "Every contribution helps AI understand sign language more accurately" types while coloured square particles drift.
    - The particles swirl in a circle and converge into a globe made of the same squares. "Accessible to millions of
      people" then types around the globe.
    - **What it says:** many small contributions become one world.
12. **48–60 s: the close.** "The future of accessible communication starts with better data." types on orange, then:
    - the marketplace logo on white
    - a cross-fade to the TalkSign logo
    - the URL typing letter by letter

## D2 devices (in order)
1. **0–3.5 s: the name types over a world that changes colour.**
   - "Meet heyclicky|" types with a real caret, centred and large. The sky behind shifts from warm peach to pink to
     lilac to blue as it types.
   - Small app icons float around the edge. The subline "an ai buddy that lives on your mac" types underneath.
   - The mascot shape sits above the name.
2. **3.6–5 s: the mascot greeting.** The cloud mascot pops in on white, and a speech bubble "hey, how can I help you?"
   types.
3. **5–6 s: zoom through a lens.**
   - A glass sphere grows around the mascot (a magnifier or bubble). It fills the frame and becomes the macOS desktop.
   - **The rule:** an object becomes the window into the next world.
4. **6–12 s: real use.**
   - App windows (anatomy, a music sequencer, maps) drift on the desktop.
   - The mascot moves between them with chat bubbles. The desktop wallpaper colour cycles.
5. **13–15 s: the script itself on screen.**
   - The narration's paragraph appears as a document; the current sentence is highlighted as it's read.
   - The camera moves through the text. **The words become the scene.**
6. **15.5–19.8 s: dots orbit around the line.**
   - "Give it a Goal" has dots in a square arrangement. "and your Clicky can help complete the work for you." is
     encircled by coloured dots in a large ring that slowly rotates and breathes.
   - **The rule:** a ring of particles frames and spotlights the sentence.
7. **19.9–23.5 s: a split panel.**
   - Left: the real UI (a grid of clicky avatars). Right: a black type card, "Create [avatars] your [avatars] own Clickys".
   - **Inline images between the words again:** small avatar clusters sit inside the sentence.
8. **23.5–29 s: feature callouts on the sky.** Small glass toast cards ("They remember their purpose", "Keep
   context", "Work alongside you") appear one at a time, centred, on a big blue cloud sky.
9. **29–33 s: the word swap with a highlight.**
   - "your [apps] / your [workflow] / your [ai buddy]" over a faint wireframe UI.
   - The swapped word arrives coloured, with a selection highlight, and a cursor flits around. Then a delete (a
     highlight selection) leaves "your buddy".
10. **33–35 s: the close.** "Meet heyclicky|" types again, now on white, with the mascot mark above it: the opening
    line reprised.

## What makes these feel "intelligent" (the transferable principles)
1. **Narration is the score.**
   - The voiceover sets the timing, and each visual change starts where a phrase starts.
   - Text on screen is the spoken words, typed live.
2. **Show the sentence.** Each line gets an image that *is* its meaning: a waveform for "learn from hours of data", a
   globe for "millions of people", a document for "understands what you're looking at".
3. **Images inside sentences.** Word-sized images sit inline between words, so the line reads as words and pictures
   together.
4. **Many become one.** A swarm of parts (particles, tags, photos, dots) converges into a single form, or one thing
   bursts into many. Use it whenever the product unifies or scales.
5. **Fixed frame, living content.** A chip, a slot, a card or a frame stays put while what's inside it changes on the
   beat. This is the montage burst again, made calmer.
6. **Objects as portals.** A sphere, a clicked card or a grown slit becomes the next world.
7. **Recurring motifs.** The binary string, the brand chip, the mascot and the caret return across the film and tie
   it together. The ending reprises the opening line.
8. **A ground per chapter, all from one palette.** D1 runs several worlds (white, plum pixel, orange grid, warm photo)
   but they all share one brand palette (orange, plum, lilac, peach, black, white). D2 keeps blue sky against white.

## Second pass: transitions, hierarchy and the camera (full frame rate)
Every transition was stepped through at 9–16 fps (`tr/`), and both cameras were measured with `camflow.py`
(`flow_D1.json`, `flow_D2.json`, `graphs_D.png`).
- **Camera:** nearly still 57% (D1) and 72% (D2) of frames, with almost no roll. D1 uses 4-frame punch-zoom pulses
  (±5–30%) on transitions; D2 uses a few smooth zooms (0.5–0.8 s).
- The 18 carrier transitions and the hierarchy rules are written up in `.claude/skills/climax-launch/TRANSITIONS.md`.
