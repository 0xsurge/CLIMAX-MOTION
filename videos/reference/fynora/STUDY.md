# Look study: Fynora (Behance case study by Abdur Razzak), sent by the user as a storyboard reference

Source: eight phone screenshots of the gallery (kept out of git). Behance blocks automated visits. The user's
brief: "not bumper to bumper", use the ideas, including the people.

## What the look is made of
1. **A bento board.** Every image is a rounded panel (radius about 4% of its width) on an off-white page (#F7F7F9),
   with narrow gaps. Panels pair up: one holds a phone, its neighbour holds one short line of text.
2. **Lots of calm space, one idea per panel.** A panel holds a phone, or a sentence, or a portrait, never all three.
3. **A giant faint logo shape** (about 4% darker than the panel) sits behind the content of almost every panel, cropped.
4. **Phones.** Real device frames, shown two ways: straight on and small (about 45% of the panel width), or as a huge
   tilted macro crop where only a corner of the phone and two or three controls are visible.
5. **Type.** One geometric sans (Urbanist) in light and regular weights. Headline about 40 px, one or two lines, centred;
   a tiny grey support line under it. A few moments switch to huge bold capitals that the phone overlaps
   ("NEW RELEASES ARE COMING SOON"), and a marquee strip repeats a phrase with a small portrait between repeats.
6. **Pill tags.** A small blue pill names each section ("Spending", "Goals", "AI Answer") at the panel's top left.
7. **Colour.** Blue to cyan gradients (#4A88F7, #12CDF7) carry the brand; orange, violet and green appear only as
   small accents. Portraits sit on a white-to-blue gradient with the logo shape behind the person.
8. **Diagrams.** Problems on the left connect by thin curved lines through the logo to solutions on the right. A user
   flow is drawn as pills joined by thin lines.
9. **People.** A smiling portrait with name and role on the gradient; a lifestyle photo of someone using a phone; a
   hand holding the phone.
10. **A billboard mockup** carries the tagline.

## How it maps to the Moniepoint film
- The film becomes a camera travelling across one big bento board; panels grow to fill the frame and shrink back.
- The dart is the giant faint shape behind panels, and the thing that travels the thin connecting lines.
- Problem lines -> the Moniepoint icon -> solution lines is the turn of the story.
- Conflict with the house rules: the reference tilts phones in 3D. The user's standing rule keeps UI square to the
  lens, so macro crops are done flat unless the user asks for tilt.
